## Answers to the brief's six blog questions

**1. Which articles support a commercial service?** 15 of 20 clearly do (integration/ZCoupler cluster is the best-represented, 7 posts). The 5 that don't cleanly: Emerging Tech in Action, Evolution of Web Development, Future of Web and App Development, EaaS (weak fit as written), and When AI Learns to Survive.

**2. Which articles are disconnected from current strategy?** Primarily **"When AI Learns to 'Survive': Alarming Signals We Can't Ignore."** This post is about third-party AI safety research (other vendors' models exhibiting deceptive behavior in tests) — it has no connection to any Zediant service, campaign, or documented capability, and its only on-page link is a weak pairing to the AI-Enabled Product Engineering service page. This is the one blog post in scope that a reader could reasonably find off-brand for a B2B engineering-services site. Secondary candidates: the three generic "web development trends" pieces (Evolution of Web Development, Future of Web/App Development, Emerging Tech in Action) — not off-brand, just generic and dated, consistent with the content audit's original finding.

**3. Which articles should link to service pages (and don't yet)?** EaaS, Empower Your Team/IT Outsourcing (already links, but to all 4 pages rather than the more relevant Dedicated Engineering specifically), and the school-website case-study post.

**4. Which important topics are missing?** See `03-content-gap-analysis.md` Section C — the staff-augmentation-vs-pods comparison is the clearest gap; otherwise the blog's topic coverage is broader than its current commercial-page set, if anything (it already covers PWA, Sitecore, and legacy-modernization angles the service pages only partially reflect).

**5. Which articles potentially compete with service pages?** None create a keyword-cannibalization risk in the classic sense (none appear to directly target a service page's exact primary keyword as their own primary target per the approved Excel file). The closer risk is topical overlap diluting a single service page's authority — e.g. three separate posts (`how-an-in-house-middleware-platform`, `why-middleware-integration-is-the-hidden-hero`, `zcoupler-vs-traditional-middleware`) all cover very similar ZCoupler-in-automotive/oil-and-gas ground. This isn't harmful, but consolidating the *linking strategy* (all three should point to `/services/middleware-integration/`, not compete with each other for attention) is worth doing when that page is expanded.

**6. Which articles are outdated?** Three from 2024 (Evolution of Web Development, Future of Web/App Development) and Emerging Tech in Action (2025 but generic) were already flagged as dated/generic in the prior content audit — reconfirmed here.

**7. Which could be consolidated?** The three ZCoupler/middleware-in-industry posts noted in point 5 cover overlapping ground and could eventually be consolidated into one deeper, canonical ZCoupler content piece — flagged as a **future consideration**, not a recommendation to delete or merge now (Section 16 of the brief: do not delete or rewrite anything in this pass).

## The single highest-priority blog finding

**13 of these 20 posts are currently unreachable from `/blogs/` (see `04-internal-linking-audit.md`).** Several of the best-targeted, most on-strategy posts — the entire ZCoupler comparison/deep-dive cluster — are among the orphaned set. Fixing blog-hub discoverability is a higher-leverage fix than writing any new blog content, since the on-strategy content mostly already exists and simply isn't reachable.
