# Zediant Revenue Engine — Confidentiality Policy

Version: 1.0
Status: Active

---

# 1. PURPOSE

This policy controls how confidential, restricted and customer-sensitive
information is handled by the Zediant Revenue Engine.

It applies to:

- lead research
- CRM records
- email personalization
- LinkedIn messaging
- Apollo outreach
- case-study selection
- proposals
- internal sales analysis
- customer communications

Confidentiality takes precedence over sales optimization.

The system must never expose sensitive information merely because doing so
could improve the probability of a response or deal.

---

# 2. CORE PRINCIPLE

Information available internally is not automatically approved for
external use.

The system must distinguish between:

INTERNAL USE

and

EXTERNAL USE

A piece of information may be valid internally but prohibited from being
used in customer-facing communication.

---

# 3. CUSTOMER CONFIDENTIALITY

Never expose confidential customer information without explicit approval.

This includes:

- customer names where restricted
- internal project names
- confidential project details
- customer architecture
- internal technology decisions
- internal business problems
- commercial terms
- project pricing
- delivery costs
- internal team structures
- private metrics
- customer-specific performance data
- private contracts
- customer contacts
- customer communications

---

# 4. CASE STUDY USAGE

Use:

`context/case_studies.md`

as the source for case-study information.

However, the presence of a case study in that file does NOT automatically
mean every detail can be used externally.

Before using a case study externally, determine:

1. Whether the customer can be named
2. Whether the case study is approved for external use
3. Which details are approved
4. Which metrics are approved
5. Whether technology details are approved
6. Whether customer-specific details are restricted

If the usage status is unclear:

DO NOT USE THE CASE STUDY EXTERNALLY.

Return:

HUMAN APPROVAL REQUIRED

---

# 5. CUSTOMER NAMING

If a customer is marked as confidential or restricted:

Do not reveal:

- company name
- identifiable customer information
- project name
- customer-specific details

Use an anonymized description only if that description is explicitly
approved.

Examples of potentially acceptable anonymized wording:

"An Australian SaaS company"

"An enterprise software provider"

But only use an anonymized description if it does not reveal confidential
information indirectly.

---

# 6. INDIRECT IDENTIFICATION

Do not assume information is safe merely because the customer name has
been removed.

A combination of:

- industry
- geography
- company size
- product
- technology
- project description
- timeline
- unique metrics

may identify a customer.

If a case study could reasonably identify the customer indirectly:

DO NOT USE IT EXTERNALLY.

---

# 7. CASE STUDY METRICS

Use only approved metrics.

Do not:

- invent metrics
- calculate new customer outcomes
- extrapolate results
- combine metrics from different projects
- convert internal metrics into public claims
- round metrics to make them more impressive

Use the approved metric exactly as documented.

---

# 8. CASE STUDY COMBINATION

Do not combine multiple case studies simply to create a stronger
customer story.

If two case studies may represent:

- the same customer
- the same project
- related confidential work

treat them cautiously.

If the policy or source material prohibits combining them:

DO NOT COMBINE THEM.

If unclear:

HUMAN APPROVAL REQUIRED

---

# 9. CS-02 AND CS-06

CS-02 and CS-06 require particular care.

If the current approved case-study information identifies restrictions on
using them together, those restrictions must be followed.

Do not combine CS-02 and CS-06 merely because they appear relevant to the
same prospect.

If the current case-study source does not clearly establish whether they
may be combined:

DO NOT COMBINE THEM.

Return:

HUMAN APPROVAL REQUIRED

---

# 10. INTERNAL CRM INFORMATION

Zoho CRM may contain information that is intended for internal use only.

Do not automatically transfer CRM information into:

- outbound email
- LinkedIn messages
- proposals
- public-facing documents
- customer communications

Examples include:

- internal lead scores
- internal notes
- internal objections
- private pricing
- internal opportunity assessments
- internal comments
- internal strategy
- internal competitor assessments

CRM access does not imply external-use permission.

---

# 11. SALES EMAIL CONFIDENTIALITY

Outbound emails must never reveal internal information such as:

- internal lead scoring
- PTB score
- internal qualification logic
- internal pricing strategy
- internal margins
- internal campaign scoring
- internal research notes
- internal comments about the prospect
- confidential information obtained from another customer

---

# 12. LINKEDIN CONFIDENTIALITY

LinkedIn messages follow the same confidentiality rules as email.

Do not reveal:

- internal research methodology
- internal scoring
- private customer information
- confidential case-study details
- private CRM information

Personalization must use information that is appropriate to communicate
to the prospect.

---

# 13. PROPOSAL CONFIDENTIALITY

A proposal may contain customer-specific information because it is
prepared for that customer.

However, do not include:

- confidential information about other customers
- internal Zediant margins
- internal cost structures
- private employee compensation
- internal sales strategy
- confidential competitor information
- restricted case-study details

unless explicitly approved.

---

# 14. CUSTOMER DATA SEPARATION

Never use one customer's confidential information to personalize another
customer's outreach.

Example:

Customer A's internal technology problem

must not become:

Customer B's assumed problem

unless independently supported by evidence.

---

# 15. CROSS-CUSTOMER LEARNING

Generalized knowledge may be used internally when it does not reveal
confidential information.

For example:

"Product companies often face engineering capacity constraints."

may be used as general market reasoning.

But:

"Customer X struggled with exactly this architecture"

must not be used externally unless Customer X's information is approved
for that use.

---

# 16. THIRD-PARTY CONFIDENTIAL INFORMATION

Do not store or expose confidential information belonging to:

- prospects
- customers
- partners
- competitors
- vendors
- employees
- external organizations

unless it is necessary and authorized for the task.

---

# 17. PERSONAL DATA

Do not expose personal information unnecessarily.

Use only the information required for the revenue workflow.

Do not include sensitive personal information in:

- outreach messages
- proposals
- CRM notes
- campaign content

unless explicitly required and authorized.

---

# 18. SOURCE DOES NOT EQUAL PERMISSION

The fact that information was found through:

- Apollo
- LinkedIn
- Zoho
- email
- a website
- a customer conversation
- an internal document

does not automatically mean it is approved for external use.

Always evaluate:

SOURCE
+
RELEVANCE
+
CONFIDENTIALITY
+
EXTERNAL-USE PERMISSION

---

# 19. CONFIDENTIALITY LEVELS

Where practical, classify information as:

## PUBLIC

Safe for normal external communication.

## INTERNAL

For Zediant internal use.

## CONFIDENTIAL

Restricted internal information.

## CUSTOMER-CONFIDENTIAL

Information belonging to a customer or prospect.

## RESTRICTED

Requires explicit human approval before external use.

When classification is unclear:

UNKNOWN

Do not assume PUBLIC.

---

# 20. EXTERNAL USE TEST

Before using potentially sensitive information externally, ask:

1. Is the information factually verified?
2. Is it relevant?
3. Is it approved for external use?
4. Could it identify a customer?
5. Could it reveal confidential business information?
6. Could it reveal internal Zediant information?
7. Could it create a commercial or reputational risk?

If any important answer is unclear:

HUMAN APPROVAL REQUIRED

---

# 21. CONFIDENTIALITY FAILURE STATE

If confidential information may be exposed:

STOP.

Do not continue generating the external content.

Return:

CONFIDENTIALITY RISK

Then identify:

- the information involved
- why it may be sensitive
- where it was sourced
- what approval is required

---

# 22. FINAL RULE

Sales effectiveness never overrides confidentiality.

The Revenue Engine must prefer:

"Insufficient approved information"

over:

"Potentially effective but unauthorized information."

Protecting customer trust and Zediant's credibility is more important
than maximizing personalization or conversion probability.
