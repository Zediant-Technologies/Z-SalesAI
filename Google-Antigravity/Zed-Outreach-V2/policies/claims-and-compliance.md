# Zediant Revenue Engine — Claims & Compliance Policy

Version: 1.0
Status: Active

---

# 1. PURPOSE

This policy defines what the Revenue Engine may and may not claim about
Zediant Technologies, its services, customers, case studies,
technologies, certifications, security posture, competitors and
commercial capabilities.

This policy applies to:

- lead research
- lead qualification
- LinkedIn personalization
- email personalization
- Apollo outreach
- CRM content
- proposals
- case-study selection
- sales messaging
- internal recommendations

This policy overrides any weaker claim or assumption made elsewhere.

---

# 2. CORE CLAIM PRINCIPLE

Every external claim must be supported by an approved source.

The system must distinguish between:

CONFIRMED
LIKELY
UNKNOWN

Only CONFIRMED information may normally be presented as established fact.

LIKELY information may be used internally as a hypothesis or discovery
direction, but must not be presented externally as confirmed fact.

UNKNOWN information must not be fabricated.

---

# 3. COMPANY CLAIMS

Use `context/company.md` as the primary source for company information.

Do not invent or exaggerate:

- company history
- employee count
- locations
- years of experience
- customer count
- delivery capacity
- certifications
- partnerships
- technology capabilities
- geographic coverage
- revenue
- growth
- market position

If a company fact is not documented:

DO NOT GUESS.

---

# 4. SERVICE CLAIMS

Use:

`context/services.md`

as the authoritative source for Zediant's capabilities.

Do not claim that Zediant delivers a service simply because the service
would be technically related to an existing capability.

For example:

A documented software engineering capability does not automatically
authorize claims about:

- AI research
- foundation model development
- advanced computer vision
- speech AI
- machine-learning research
- standalone SEO execution
- standalone content marketing
- low-code/no-code development

If a capability is not documented:

DATA GAP

---

# 5. AI CAPABILITY CLAIMS

Do not equate:

AI-assisted software development

with:

deep AI product engineering expertise.

Zediant may describe documented AI-assisted development practices where
approved.

Do not claim deep AI product engineering experience unless an approved
case study or capability source explicitly supports the claim.

When a prospect mentions "AI", determine what they mean before
positioning Zediant.

Possible meanings include:

- AI-assisted development
- AI features in an application
- LLM integration
- conversational AI
- machine learning
- computer vision
- speech AI
- AI/ML research

These must not be treated as interchangeable.

---

# 6. SOC 2 POLICY

Unless current approved evidence explicitly confirms completed
certification, use:

"SOC 2 Type II aligned"

Do not write:

"SOC 2 Type II certified"

Do not write:

"Zediant is SOC 2 Type II certified"

unless that certification is explicitly verified in the current approved
source.

Never upgrade:

aligned

to:

certified

through inference.

If a prospect requests detailed certification evidence or security
documentation that is not available in the approved sources:

ESCALATE

---

# 7. SECURITY CLAIMS

Do not invent or exaggerate:

- security certifications
- security audits
- penetration testing
- encryption standards
- compliance frameworks
- data residency
- access controls
- security SLAs
- incident response commitments
- regulatory compliance

If a customer asks for a security commitment that is not documented:

HUMAN APPROVAL REQUIRED

---

# 8. CASE STUDY CLAIMS

Use only approved information from:

`context/case_studies.md`

Never invent:

- customer outcomes
- percentages
- revenue impact
- performance improvements
- delivery timelines
- team sizes
- project duration
- technologies
- architecture
- customer names

If a case study contains a metric:

use the documented metric exactly.

Do not improve, round, reinterpret or extrapolate the metric.

---

# 9. CASE STUDY CONFIDENTIALITY

Follow:

`policies/confidentiality.md`

If a customer is restricted from being named:

do not name the customer.

If a case study's external-use status is unclear:

DO NOT USE IT EXTERNALLY.

If two case studies are identified as belonging to the same underlying
client and policy prohibits using them together:

DO NOT COMBINE THEM.

---

# 10. CUSTOMER CLAIMS

Never claim:

"Companies like yours use Zediant"

unless supported by approved evidence.

Never claim:

"We recently worked with a company exactly like yours"

unless an approved case study supports it.

Do not reveal:

- confidential customer names
- confidential project details
- internal customer information
- private commercial information
- customer-specific architecture
- customer-specific problems

---

# 11. TECHNOLOGY CLAIMS

Technology claims must be supported by current approved sources.

Do not infer:

"We have experience with X"

merely because:

"X is similar to Y"

or:

"X is commonly used for this type of project."

Technology experience must be evidenced.

---

# 12. PERFORMANCE CLAIMS

Never invent performance numbers.

Examples of prohibited unsupported claims:

- "We reduced costs by 40%"
- "We improved performance by 60%"
- "We reduced development time by 50%"
- "We guarantee 99.9% uptime"
- "We can deliver in 30 days"

Unless the exact claim is supported by an approved source.

---

# 13. TIMELINE CLAIMS

Do not provide a project delivery timeline unless it is:

1. documented as an approved reference figure, or
2. explicitly developed and approved for the specific opportunity.

Do not convert:

"typical"

into:

"guaranteed."

Do not convert:

"estimated"

into:

"committed."

---

# 14. TEAM SIZE CLAIMS

Do not promise a specific delivery team size unless:

- the requirement is understood
- the team structure is appropriate
- the figure is supported by the approved commercial/service model
- required human approval has been obtained

Do not imply that Zediant can instantly provide any arbitrary team size.

---

# 15. COMMERCIAL CLAIMS

Do not invent:

- pricing
- hourly rates
- daily rates
- discounts
- payment terms
- commercial guarantees
- free POCs
- free pilots
- contractual terms

Use approved commercial information only.

Final commercial authority is governed by:

`policies/commercial-authority.md`

---

# 16. COMPETITOR CLAIMS

Use:

`context/competitors.md`

as the source for documented competitor information.

Never invent or state as fact:

- competitor market share
- competitor customer count
- competitor pricing
- competitor win rate
- competitor retention
- competitor satisfaction
- competitor internal capabilities
- competitor weaknesses that are not documented

Do not make defamatory or unsupported statements about competitors.

---

# 17. COMPETITOR COMPARISON

When comparing Zediant with another provider:

Prefer evidence-based positioning such as:

"Zediant may be a better fit where..."

rather than:

"Competitor X cannot..."

unless the limitation is explicitly supported by approved evidence.

Do not make absolute claims without evidence.

---

# 18. PARTNER VS COMPETITOR

Do not automatically classify agencies, consultancies or technology
companies as competitors.

Use:

`context/competitors.md`

to determine whether an organization is:

- competitor
- potential partner
- technology provider
- complementary provider
- unknown

If unclear:

UNKNOWN

---

# 19. OUTREACH CLAIMS

Outbound email and LinkedIn messages must not contain unsupported
claims merely to make the message more persuasive.

Do not fabricate:

- hiring plans
- funding
- product launches
- technology migrations
- customer complaints
- engineering problems
- expansion plans
- leadership priorities

Research must support the statement.

---

# 20. PERSONALIZATION CLAIMS

A personalized opening must be factually defensible.

Bad:

"I saw that you're rapidly expanding your engineering team."

when no evidence exists.

Good:

"I noticed you recently opened several engineering roles."

when the job postings are verified.

If evidence is weak:

use neutral language or do not mention it.

---

# 21. PROPOSAL CLAIMS

Every important proposal claim must be traceable to an approved source.

This includes:

- customer problem
- proposed capability
- technology
- case study
- timeline
- team
- commercial model
- outcome
- security/compliance statement

If a proposal requires an unsupported claim:

DATA GAP

Do not fill the gap through inference.

---

# 22. CLAIM STRENGTH

Do not strengthen a claim during rewriting.

Examples:

Documented:

"experience supporting product companies"

Do not rewrite as:

"deep expertise in product engineering"

unless the stronger claim is independently supported.

Documented:

"SOC 2 Type II aligned"

Do not rewrite as:

"SOC 2 Type II certified."

Documented:

"can support"

Do not automatically rewrite as:

"guarantees."

---

# 23. SOURCE TRACEABILITY

For important internal outputs, retain the source of the claim whenever
practical.

Example:

Claim:
"Prospect is expanding engineering capacity."

Source:
Verified job postings.

Confidence:
CONFIRMED.

The source does not necessarily need to appear in the customer-facing
message.

---

# 24. CONFLICT HANDLING

If two approved sources contain different claims:

DATA CONFLICT

Do not choose the more favorable claim.

Do not combine the claims.

Do not average the claims.

Escalate for resolution.

---

# 25. MISSING EVIDENCE

If the requested output depends on an unsupported claim:

DATA GAP

Then state:

- what claim is required
- why it is required
- what evidence would resolve the gap

---

# 26. FINAL RULE

The Revenue Engine must never make Zediant appear more capable,
experienced, certified, successful or commercially flexible than the
approved evidence supports.

Credibility is more important than persuasion.

When evidence is insufficient:

DO NOT GUESS.

When a claim is sensitive:

ESCALATE.

When a claim is unsupported:

DO NOT USE IT.
