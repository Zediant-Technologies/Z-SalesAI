# Enterprise Custom Development — Re-Audit (Strategic, Client, Content-SEO & UX)
**Reviewed:** live page at https://zediant.com/services/enterprise-custom-development/, checked just now via live browser session (your screenshot matched what I pulled — a WebFetch attempt on this same URL first returned stale content that didn't match either, and was discarded rather than used).
**Scope:** Strategic messaging, buyer experience, content-level SEO, and section structure — same lens as the other three audits. No technical SEO, design, or dev work in scope.

---

## Three direct answers

**1. What's already strong — and what genuinely improved**
This page has been substantially rebuilt, not just patched, and it's a real improvement. The old "Bespoke Engineering Solutions for Public Limited Companies (PLCs)" hero is gone entirely, replaced with "Build and modernize enterprise software around your business" — no jargon, no ambiguity. Two new structural additions stand out: a "Standard Software vs. Enterprise Custom Development" comparison (mirroring the same differentiator pattern now used on the Platform Engineering page's "vs. DevOps" table — a good sign of a consistent site-wide improvement, not a one-off), and a "Build New / Modernize Existing" dual-path section with two genuine five-step methodologies, which gives this page a real answer to "what does working with you actually look like" that it didn't have before. The old "Enterprise Engineering Challenges" section replaced a listicle of "hurdles" with a "the complexity / how we address it" structure for each of four problems — more specific and more credible. A case-studies section ("Enterprise Engineering in Practice") now exists where none did before, and the "Fort Knox" metaphor is gone from the contact section, which I confirmed directly by reading the live page's script-rendered content, not just the visible screenshot.

**2. What's actually undermining the page now**
Two of the four original P1 issues are still there, unchanged, word for word. The "offshore" self-framing is untouched: *"In the enterprise sector, 'Offshore' is often seen as a risk. Zediant changes that narrative through three core pillars of reliability."* This still directly conflicts with the "embedded extension of your team" positioning fixed elsewhere on the site. And CTA phrasing is still inconsistent — "Consult with our Enterprise Team" (hero), "Discuss Your Enterprise Project," "Consult with Our Engineering Experts," and "Talk to our engineering team" (form button) are four different phrasings for what's functionally the same action, unchanged from the original audit.

**3. What needs improvement**
The new case-studies section has the same credibility gap flagged on the Platform Engineering re-audit: it's titled "Enterprise Engineering in Practice," but the two case studies under it are tagged "Dedicated Engineering Pods" and "AI-Enabled Product Engineering" — neither is tagged, or reads, as Enterprise Custom Development work. That's now a pattern across at least two of the four service pages, not a one-off, and worth fixing at the source (either genuinely enterprise-tagged case studies, or section titles that don't claim a specificity the proof doesn't back up). Separately, the old "Specialized Knowledge for Complex Industries" section — four industry-specific cards (Automotive & CRM, Fintech, Logistics, Enterprise E-commerce) — has been removed entirely rather than just having its one unverified claim (the 30% UAE stat) softened. That resolves the original issue, but it also removes real industry-specific proof and segmentation language the page used to have; worth a deliberate call on whether that's a net improvement or a gap to fill differently.

## PART 3 — Issues Still Open

**"Offshore" framing — unresolved, identical to the original audit.** *"In the enterprise sector, 'Offshore' is often seen as a risk. Zediant changes that narrative through three core pillars of reliability."* Same recommendation as before: *"In the enterprise sector, trust has to be earned, not assumed. Zediant builds that trust through three core pillars of reliability."* This keeps the section's structure (name an objection, counter it) without Zediant labeling itself "offshore," which still conflicts with the "embedded extension of your team" positioning used elsewhere on the site — including, notably, this page's own new "Built for Evolution" / "Integration-Ready" language just above it, which reads like a genuine partner, not an offshore vendor.

**CTA phrasing — still not standardized.** Four distinct phrasings remain: "Consult with our Enterprise Team" (hero), "Discuss Your Enterprise Project," "Consult with Our Engineering Experts," and "Talk to our engineering team" (form button). Same recommendation as before: pick one — e.g., "Talk to Our Enterprise Engineering Team" — and use it everywhere on this page.

**Case-studies section proves the wrong thing.** "Enterprise Engineering in Practice" is illustrated by the Wholesale Parts CRM (tagged "Dedicated Engineering Pods") and the Lubricant Recommendation Tool (tagged "AI-Enabled Product Engineering") — the same two case studies used identically on the Platform Engineering page, with the same tag mismatch. This is now a confirmed sitewide pattern, not a page-specific slip: reused proof, presented under a section title that implies specificity the tags directly contradict. Worth fixing once, structurally — either genuinely enterprise-tagged case studies, or section headers across all pages that don't claim a specificity the two available stories can't back up.

**Trade-off worth a deliberate decision: the industry-specific proof points are gone.** The old "Specialized Knowledge for Complex Industries" section (Automotive & CRM, Fintech, Logistics, Enterprise E-commerce) is no longer on the page at all. This resolves the original unverified-claim issue, but it also removes the only industry-segmentation language and vertical-specific credibility markers the page had — worth knowing whether that was a deliberate simplification or something to reintroduce in a version that doesn't restate unverified numbers.

**The remaining "30–40% faster" mention sits oddly.** It's now down to one occurrence (good), but that occurrence — an "AI-Enabled Engineering Efficiency" card — sits inside the "three core pillars of reliability" trust section, as an uncounted fourth item answering a different question (speed, not trust/reliability) than the other three cards (governance, SOC 2, global footprint). Minor, but worth a look if this section gets revisited.
