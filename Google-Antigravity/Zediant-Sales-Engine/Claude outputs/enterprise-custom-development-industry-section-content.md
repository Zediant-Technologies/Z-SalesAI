# Reintroducing Industry-Specific Proof — Draft Copy
**For:** Enterprise Custom Development page
**Purpose:** Restore the industry-segmentation language and vertical-specific credibility the old "Specialized Knowledge for Complex Industries" section carried, without restating the unverified "30% efficiency increase for major UAE automotive partners" claim (or any other uncited number) that got it removed in the first place.

A flag before the copy: I've kept every claim below to things that are either already stated elsewhere on your site (the SOC 2/global-footprint language) or phrased as capability rather than a specific track-record number. If you *do* have a real, citable metric for any of these verticals — a specific client result you can stand behind — swap it in; a genuine number always beats a general capability statement. I haven't invented any stats to fill the gaps.

## Intro line

**Recommended:** "Enterprise software looks different depending on the industry it runs in. Our experience across these sectors means we understand not just the technology, but the operational and compliance realities specific to your business."

**Why:** This keeps the original's framing (industry context matters, not just technical skill) without the phrase "navigate the technical and compliance hurdles unique to your industry," which implied a track record the old cards then tried to back up with the unverified number. This version makes a capability claim, not a results claim.

## Four industry cards

### Automotive & Dealer Networks
**Recommended:** "Engineering CRM, DMS integration and data infrastructure for automotive dealer networks and OEM-scale operations — built to handle high transaction volumes and multi-dealer reporting."

**Why this instead of the old version:** The original ("We've delivered a 30% efficiency increase for major UAE automotive partners") was the specific claim flagged as uncited. This version keeps the real substance you *do* have proof for — you already have a genuine, detailed case study here (the Wholesale Parts CRM build, with actual results: 50+ dealers onboarded, 5,700+ customers) — without asserting an efficiency percentage nobody can check. If you want to lean on that real case study directly from this card, consider linking it: "...built to handle high transaction volumes and multi-dealer reporting — see how in our Wholesale Parts CRM case study" with a link to that case study.

### Fintech & Transactional Platforms
**Recommended:** "Building low-latency systems designed for data integrity and high-volume transaction processing, where reliability isn't optional."

**Why:** Nearly identical to the original — this one didn't have an unverified number attached, so it needed the least change. Kept for consistency of tone with the other three cards.

### Logistics & Supply Chain
**Recommended:** "Creating visibility across global networks through real-time tracking, data integration and operational reporting."

**Why:** Also close to the original — "intelligent, real-time tracking and optimization" softened slightly to "real-time tracking, data integration and operational reporting," since "optimization" on its own implies a measurable outcome the page doesn't back up elsewhere.

### Enterprise E-commerce
**Recommended:** "Architecting multi-region platforms built to handle high-concurrency traffic and peak-demand scaling."

**Why:** The original claimed "millions of concurrent users" as an established fact. This version keeps the ambition (built for scale, high concurrency) as a capability the engineering approach targets, without stating a specific user-count figure that isn't tied to a named client or system.
